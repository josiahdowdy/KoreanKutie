The memory trick this app runs on

Seven sound traps (read this once, save weeks)

eo = “uh”

The romanization lies. 서울 (Seoul) = “suh-ool”, never “see-oh”. 예뻐 = ye-ppuh.

eu = “euh”

The flat grunt — say “oo” with unrounded, flat lips. The sound of lifting groceries.

ㄹ = r + l

One quick tongue-tap on the roof of the mouth, halfway between r and l. Don't roll it, don't hold it.

twins = tense

ㅃ ㄸ ㄲ ㅆ ㅉ are squeezed, with zero puff of air. Palm test: 파 puffs on your hand, 빠 doesn't.

s → “sh”

ㅅ turns to sh before i/y sounds: 싶어 = “shee-puh”, not “see-puh”.

ㅇ two jobs

Silent placeholder at the front of a block; a hummed “ng” in the basement. 안녕 uses both.

soft endings

Final consonants are unreleased: 밥 (bap) ends with lips closed — no “puh” escapes.

Reading blocks 읽기

il-gi

Korean doesn't write letters in a row — it stacks them into neat square syllable blocks, like Lego. Consonant + vowel, sometimes with a consonant in the basement. Master the stack, and you can sound out any Korean word on Earth.

The three stack shapes

The vowel decides the layout: tall vowels (ㅏ ㅓ ㅣ …) stand to the right; flat vowels (ㅗ ㅜ ㅡ) lie below. A final consonant (받침 bat-chim, “the support”) moves into the basement.

One dial: how sweet vs. how polite

Korean changes its endings depending on who you're talking to. Good news: with your wife you use banmal — the intimate casual form — which is also the shortest. Golden rule: adding ~요 (yo) to a phrase makes it polite for everyone else.

Aegyo 애교 — weaponized cuteness

Aegyo is Korea's beloved art of acting cute on purpose. It's how you say it:

stretch it

사랑해 → 사랑해애애~ (sarang-heeeh~). Dragging the last vowel = instant cute.

pitch up

Go higher and softer than your normal voice. Add a head tilt. Commit.

the classic

Learn the viral “Gwiyomi song” finger-counting game (1 + 1 = 귀요미!) for guaranteed giggles.
